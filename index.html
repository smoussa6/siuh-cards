Swan-Ganz Catheter Placement

Edwards PA catheter via MAC 9Fr introducer

▼

Setup

  1. Obtain consent.
    📋 Paper consent document — found in the CCU.
  2. Position: Supine, slight Trendelenburg, head turned left.
  3. Place Zoll pads on patient and ensure telemetry functional.
  4. Full sterile prep — cap, mask, gown, gloves, full-body drape. ChloraPrep the site and allow to dry.
  5. Time out.

Introducer Sheath Placement

  1. US-guided R IJ access. Confirm compressibility, non-pulsatile. Aspirate venous blood to confirm.
  2. Confirm venous return. Dark, non-pulsatile.
  3. J-wire (0.035") — advance smoothly under ultrasound visualization. Confirm wire in vein. Watch for ectopy.
  4. Nick skin. Advance dilator/sheath over wire. Never let go of wire.
  5. Remove dilator + wire. Confirm venous blood. Aspirate and flush all ports.
  6. Secure hubbed catheter with sutures.

Swan Catheter Advancement

  1. Prepare Swan-Ganz Catheter:
    • Inflate balloon with air → deflate to confirm function
    • Flush all lumens
    • Place sterile sheath ("Swandom") before advancing
    • Attach 2 blue claves to extra ports
  2. Zero transducer.
    💡 Level
    Ensure transducer is level with the phlebostatic axis (4th intercostal space, midaxillary line).
  3. Insert catheter DEFLATED to ~20 cm (should be near RA).
  4. Inflate balloon with 1.5 mL of air. Advance with balloon inflated.
  5. RV (~30-35 cm) — note RV waveform on monitor. Move quickly.
  6. PA (~40-45 cm) — diastolic step-up. Dicrotic notch.
  7. Wedge (~45-55 cm) — PCWP tracing obtained. Record pressure and distance.
  8. Deflate balloon, observe PA waveform return, and withdraw 2–5 cm. Leave deflated in PA.
  9. Note depth. Secure. CXR. Document.
🚨 Critical Reminders
Never withdraw catheter with balloon inflated. Never inject liquid into the balloon port.

💡 Can't advance past 30?
Coiling in RA. Deflate, pull back to 20 cm, re-inflate, and re-attempt.
💡 RV Ectopy
  • Advance briskly through the RV — do not linger
  • VT/VF → immediately deflate balloon and withdraw catheter
⚠️ Overwedging
Balloon >15 sec → PA rupture. Fatal.
🚨 PA Rupture
Hemoptysis → deflate balloon, side of rupture DOWN, stop anticoagulation, interventional radiology STAT consult, +/- CT surgery.
💡 Clotted Swan
Step 1: Heparinized saline flush — from pharmacy, 100 U/mL for 3 mL flush.

Step 2 (if Step 1 fails): CathFlo 2 mg/mL for 2 mL flush.
  • Constitute solution
  • Disconnect all catheters (ports)
  • Flush into the PA distal catheter
  • Dwell for 1–2 hours
  • Aspirate as much as possible
  • "Power" flush with 3 saline flushes
  • Reconnect all
  • CXR — Zone 3
  • Zero transducer each shift
  • Waveform check each shift
  • Never flush while wedged
  • Daily reassess need

Transvenous Pacemaker (TVP)

Emergency temporary pacing • R IJ or femoral

▼
⚠️ Bridge
If unstable → transcutaneous pads while setting up.
  1. Venous introducer (≥6 Fr) — R IJ preferred. US-guided, sterile.
  2. Connect wire to generator BEFORE inserting. VVI, rate ~80, output at least 10 mA.
  3. Advance catheter through the sheath. Once passed 20 cm, inflate balloon and advance.
  4. RV capture (~35-40 cm) — spike → wide QRS, LBBB.
  5. Find threshold — deflate balloon. Slowly decrease output until capture is lost (= threshold). Should be <1-2 mA.
  6. Output at 2-3× threshold (minimum 5 mA).
  7. Check sensitivity. Half sensing threshold.
  8. Secure. CXR. Document threshold, rate, and output settings.
🚨 Loss of Capture?
↑ output → CXR → reposition. Effusion/diaphragmatic pacing → perforation → echo.

IABP Removal at Bedside

Femoral • ACT ≤ 150

▼

Before

  1. Confirm with attending.
  2. Wean per attending (1:1 → 1:2 → 1:3 as tolerated per hemodynamics).
  3. ACT ≤ 150. Hold heparin ~2-4 hrs. Use cath lab ACT machine if CCU machine is down.
  4. Premedicate patient with pain medications if no contraindication. Ensure atropine, pressor, and IV fluids are at bedside/available.

Removal

  1. Obtain sterile gloves, gown, and mask with face-shield. Coordinate with other available fellows if possible.
  2. Remove dressing and cut sutures.
  3. Turn IABP off. Press "Inflate/Deflate" to ensure balloon is fully deflated.
  4. Withdraw slowly. When initial resistance is felt, pull both sheath and balloon pump together. Don't re-advance.
  5. Allow brief bleeding to flush the site → firm manual pressure 1-2 cm above skin puncture.
  6. Hold pressure: 5–6 minutes per Fr.
  7. Dressing. Bed rest 4-6 hrs.
💡 Holding Pressure Protocol
First 10–15 minutes: Occlusive, firm pressure.
Thereafter: Firm pressure — refer to pulse oximetry waveform on the ipsilateral extremity to guide.
🚨 Can't withdraw?
STOP. Notify CCU attending and interventionalist.
⚠️ Inspect catheter
Fragment missing → call attending. Check distal pulses (DP/PT) before and after removal.

Femoral Arterial Sheath Pull

ACT ≤ 150 • 3-5 min/Fr • Pulse ox on toe

▼

Before

  • ACT ≤ 150
  • Atropine 1 mg IV + 1L NS at bedside
  • Baseline vitals, pulses, inspect groin/abdomen/genitalia
  • Pulse ox on ipsilateral toe
  • Auto BP q5 min
  • HOB ≤ 12 in. Flat if on GP IIb/IIIa.

Pull

  1. Sterile gloves. Cut sutures.
  2. Palpate femoral pulse above sheath.
  3. Withdraw slowly. Pressure 2-3 fingers slightly proximal/medial to arteriotomy.
  4. Inspect catheter. Intact? Clots?

Radial Band Removal

TR Band / Prelude Sync • 1 mL/sec × 5 → 4 mL q15 min • Max 18 mL

▼
  1. Confirm no bleeding.
  2. Non-sterile gloves. Syringe to valve.
  3. Remove 1 mL/sec for first 5 mL. Watch site.
  4. Bleeding? Re-inject 2 mL → wait 30 min → retry.
  5. No bleeding? 4 mL q15 min until empty.
  6. Remove band. Dress site.

Monitor neurovascular. BP opposite arm. Post-removal: Q4h.

🚨 Escalate if:
Uncontrolled bleeding, hand pain, numbness/coldness.

🚫 Absolute Contraindications

  • Perforated viscus
  • Esophageal stricture
  • Esophageal tumor
  • Esophageal perforation / laceration
  • Esophageal diverticulum
  • Active upper GI bleed
  • Cervical spinal instability

⚠️ Relative Contraindications

  • History of radiation to neck and mediastinum
  • History of GI surgery
  • Recent upper GI bleed
  • Barrett's esophagus
  • History of dysphagia
  • Restriction of neck mobility (severe cervical arthritis, atlantoaxial joint disease)
  • Symptomatic hiatal hernia
  • Esophageal varices
  • Coagulopathy / thrombocytopenia
  • Active esophagitis
  • Active peptic ulcer disease
  • Active respiratory issues with significant hypoxia and/or wheezing
  • Loose teeth
⚠️ Clinical Note
Patients with relative contraindications should be evaluated on a case-by-case basis. If GI pathology may pose a risk, obtain GI clearance prior to proceeding. Radiation, scleroderma, and achalasia may affect esophageal tissue and increase perforation risk.

Highlights:

  • Post-PCI STEMI with Zwolle ≤3 → safe for telemetry (mortality 0-0.5%)
  • Stable NSTEMI → only 14% develop CCU-level complications
  • 81% of ADHF don't need CCU therapies in first 48 hrs
  • SCAI C-E shock → always CCU
  • All acute aortic syndromes → CCU initially
  • Uncomplicated post-TAVR → CCU not needed (0.9% mortality)
